## Short narrative — what matters most

GoodBefore does not have a feature-deficit problem yet. It has an unproven activation/retention problem. The strongest independent evidence I found supports the core behavior you have already built: make household inventory visible, surface what needs attention, and reduce the effort of maintaining that inventory. In a 2025 intervention involving 119 Japanese households, the food-management-app condition produced a 29% reduction in measured food waste over the intervention period; the stronger effects were associated with improved inventory knowledge and avoiding over-buying. Importantly, notifications by themselves were weaker: although 56.7% said they used food after a notification, the study did not find a statistically significant improvement in prioritizing foods solely from the notification mechanism. Systematic reviews reach a similar conclusion: information alone is insufficient; interventions work better when embedded in routines and repeated touchpoints. (MDPI: https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/14/6392)

So the order I would use is stability → measurement → monetisation plumbing → household → retention intelligence → AI. First, ship v1.1.1, remove the FreshTrack/GoodBefore brand mismatch, verify billing end-to-end and get real people through the existing product. Do not start building a recipe system, fridge vision, generic AI assistant, or broad shopping product before knowing whether receipt/barcode/manual capture is sufficiently painless. Logging cost is the existential risk of this category: in the intervention above, only 50% reported registering newly purchased food and 43.3% registered existing fridge items. Fango's entire current positioning is essentially an attack on that problem: receipt in, fridge populated, minimum typing. (MDPI, as above)

Your proposed $3.99 monthly / $19.99 yearly, household included is reasonable enough to test rather than reduce pre-emptively. Fango is $2.99/$22.99 with four-person household sharing; KitchenPal is $3.99/$14.99; CozZo charged $17.99/year for household; AnyList is $14.99/year for its household tier. NoWaste is only $6.99/year, but achieves that partly by gating scanner/list/item capacity, exactly the model you do not want. RevenueCat's 2026 data also shows annual subscriptions retain substantially better at one year than monthly ones, while long trials of 17–32 days have higher median trial-to-paid conversion than very short trials. Because GoodBefore is freemium and its Premium benefit emerges only after somebody has a kitchen worth syncing, I would not start the trial at install: start a 30-day Premium trial when someone first requests Backup, joins/invites a household, or otherwise reaches a server-dependent feature. (Fango: https://fango.fi/en/)

The retention architecture should then be explicit: D1 = get a real kitchen into the app; D7 = rescue something; D30 = learn something about your household; D180 = GoodBefore knows the household well enough that starting over elsewhere has a real cost. The Sunday Statement, household sharing, waste hotspots, price history and personalized shelf-life estimates create that progression. Only after those loops exist would I add generative AI. The first AI feature should be the already-planned Rescue Plan, not an assistant tab: answer a concrete question using structured inventory the app already possesses. On compatible Android devices, Gemini Nano can now do text and image prompting locally with no per-call cloud cost; cloud intelligence can remain Premium for broader device coverage and higher quality. (Google for Developers: https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai/prompt/android)

## The implementation sequence I would actually give the coding AI

For the next release cycle, I would scope only: v1.1.1 production release, GoodBefore store rename, Play billing products + purchase/restore verification, the eight-metric event specification, three-item activation, rating timing, and—if it does not jeopardize the release—Freeze. Then get the first real cohort instead of extending the roadmap.

The first substantial product sprint after billing should be Household. Immediately after Household, ship the Sunday Statement and Household Activity because together they give Premium a recurring reason to exist rather than making sync merely an insurance policy. At that point your $19.99 annual proposition becomes much easier to explain: one shared kitchen that remembers everything.

After that, build the pieces that compound with data: Waste Hotspots → honest price/value history → learned household timing. Those are more strategically important than generic AI because they become better specifically because the user has been using GoodBefore.

Then build Rescue Plan as the first generative feature. On-device where the device supports it; cloud Premium where it does not or where higher-quality reasoning is useful. Current Android's Gemini Nano direction is particularly compatible with your privacy position: Google explicitly positions it for low-cost, offline, privacy-preserving inference, although Prompt API support is constrained by Android/AICore/device availability and cannot replace your conventional flows. (Android AI overview: https://developer.android.com/ai/overview)

One benchmark warning: do not panic when retention looks low. Food & Drink Android apps are reported around only ~2.9% D30 retention in one 2026 compilation of AppsFlyer data, while task-specific utilities also have steep curves; definitions vary substantially between providers. More useful for GoodBefore is whether activated kitchens retain, not whether every random install returns. RevenueCat's data gives the same warning on monetisation: median Google Play D35 download-to-paid is only about 0.9% globally across subscription apps, while once users actually start a trial, Google Play trial-to-paid performance is comparable to iOS. Your job therefore is not maximizing raw installs; it is getting the right person from install → real inventory → first rescue → recurring household value → Premium intent. (Braze on mobile app retention: https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/mobile-app-retention)
